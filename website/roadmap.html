CE and compliance for imported robots

Importing robots from outside the EU, renting them out under our name or changing them with our gateway makes us the importer or manufacturer under the EU Machinery Regulation 2023/1230. No robot may run in a clinic without full CE conformity.

Months 1–3

1 · Purpose and supplier audit

Non-medical logistics and assistance only (no patient lifting, diagnosis or therapy), so no MDR approval. OEM test reports for batteries (UN 38.3, IEC 62133-2), RoHS/REACH, emergency stop and torque limits.

Months 4–9

2 · Risk assessment

EN ISO 12100 hazard analysis for busy clinic corridors; EN ISO 13482 and ISO/TS 15066 for service robots and arms; retrofit hard-wired emergency stop or speed limits where missing.

Months 10–16

3 · Lab tests

Electrical safety (EN 60204-1, EN 62368-1, DGUV V3), EMC including EN 60601-1-2 so patient monitors are not disturbed, radio (RED, EN 18031).

Months 13–20

4 · Cyber, AI and privacy

Cyber Resilience Act file with SBOM and vulnerability handling; AI Act transparency light and human-in-the-loop; template DPIA; ISO 27001 and BSI C5 for the cloud.

Months 18–24

5 · CE declaration

Technical file (kept 10 years), German and Danish manuals, CE mark and our address as EU importer; signed EU declaration of conformity.

Critical path

3 biggest risks

Lab waiting times

Accredited EMC and radio labs book 4–6 months ahead.

Plan: book test slots for months 14–15 already in month 6; cheap pre-compliance scan in month 12.

Missing OEM documents

Non-EU robot makers may not hand over battery, radio and test reports.

Plan: make full reports and the ROS 2 driver source a payment condition in the first purchase contract.

Pilots waiting for Gen 2

Certification of our own gateway must not delay revenue in year 1.

Plan: run the pilots on a CE-certified European robot platform with the certified off-the-shelf gateway; real clinic data from month 4.
