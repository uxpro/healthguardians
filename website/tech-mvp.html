Tech. MVP

What runs today, end to end: a 3D-printed robot head with a phone as its face talks with a patient, every step of the conversation lands in the database within seconds, and the clinic and logistics dashboards show it live. The phone app stands in for the Sovereign Care Gateway: it plays the gateway's role, without the gateway's hardware security.

Roha2212: the hardware

working prototype
  1. Head3D-printed in PLA, 169 mm wide, with the HG logo, a phone dock with snap latches and access windows for the coupler screws.
  2. Face: Motorola Edge 60 FusionRuns the HealthGuardians app (Flutter): honeycomb eyes in four modes (idling, speaking, listening, action) and the recorded voice of Roha2212.
  3. Neck couplerPrinted sleeve and fin on the motor shaft; end stops in the head limit the turn.
  4. Stepper motorNEMA 17 (17HM19-2004S) on an 80 mm printed mount, turning the head left and right.
  5. Controller (below the picture)Arduino Due with a RADDS v1.6 board and a TMC2209 driver; an AccelStepper sketch makes calm, randomised head turns with pauses of 2–4.5 s.
